A Festival Born of a River

Why Patna. Why the Ganga.

"Every civilisation is born at a riverbank. Ours was born here."

The Ganga does not merely flow — she speaks. For five thousand years she has carried the words of sages, the songs of poets, the debates of philosophers, the prayers of millions. It is only fitting that India's newest and most purposeful literature festival should find its home on her banks — in Patna, ancient Pataliputra, capital of empires, birthplace of Chandragupta, Chanakya's laboratory of governance, Sher Shah Suri's city, Guru Gobind Singh's city.


The Ganga Literature Festival is not simply a gathering of writers. It is a civilisational conversation. A place where authors, thinkers, historians, scientists, entrepreneurs, and artists come together to ask the questions that matter most: Who are we? Where do we come from? And where — together — can we go?


Conceived and presented by BIHAAN — a movement for the Rising Bharat — in partnership with BluOne Ink, India's most purposeful publishing house, and in collaboration with SPIC MACAY, the movement that has brought classical heritage to every corner of India, the Ganga Literature Festival is Bihar's gift to the nation's intellectual life.

Our Story

About the Festival

India's civilisation has always been a conversation. GLF is the next chapter.

The Ganga Literature Festival was conceived from a simple conviction: that Bihar — home to Pataliputra, Nalanda, Vikramashila, and Bodh Gaya — deserves a literature festival commensurate with its extraordinary intellectual and spiritual heritage.


Bihar is not the periphery of India's story. It is its centre. The Arthashastra was written here. The first pan-Indian empire was administered from here. The Buddha attained enlightenment here. Guru Gobind Singh was born here. In the great tapestry of Indian civilisation, Bihar's thread runs gold.


And yet, for too long, Bihar's voice in India's national intellectual conversation has been muted. The Ganga Literature Festival changes that. It brings the ideas, the authors, the debates, and the art to Patna — and invites the nation to listen.

"The Ganga is not merely a river. She is our oldest library. We do not merely hold a festival on her banks. We open her pages."

BIHAAN — meaning dawn, or new beginning — is a movement dedicated to connecting India's vision for growth with global partnerships, community, culture, causes, and events. BIHAAN believes that a rising Bharat requires not just economic growth but civilisational confidence: a people who know their history, own their identity, and dream boldly.


BluOne Ink is India's fastest-growing publishing house with a purpose — bringing to readers the books that India needs to read. Their catalogue spans national security, civilisational history, political philosophy, Dharmic knowledge systems, fiction, and biography. Over 150 authors. Over 300 titles. All purposeful.


SPIC MACAY — the Society for the Promotion of Indian Classical Music And Culture Amongst Youth — has, for nearly five decades, been taking the best of India's classical arts heritage to schools, colleges, and communities across the nation and the world. Their evening concerts at GLF will be experiences not to be forgotten.

Our Mission

What GLF Stands For

The Ganga Literature Festival stands at the intersection of three urgent imperatives of our time.

Reclaiming Narrative
India's history has been filtered through colonial and ideological lenses for too long. GLF creates space for authentic, evidence-based, civilisation-affirming scholarship.
Accelerating Viksit Bharat
Every great nation is built first in the minds of its people. GLF creates the intellectual architecture for PM Modi's dream of a fully developed India by 2047.
Bihar Reborn
GLF places Patna back at the centre of India's intellectual map, honouring Bihar's heritage and its immense potential as a driver of India's next chapter.

Where We Gather

Venue & Location

"In the ancient world, every great conversation happened near a river. We return to that tradition."

The Ganga Literature Festival takes place in Patna, Bihar — on or near the banks of the Ganga, in a venue that honours the sacred geography of this ancient city.


Patna is Pataliputra — arguably the greatest city the ancient world ever produced. Capital of the Maurya Empire. Home of Chanakya's Arthashastra. City of Guru Gobind Singh Ji. In the colonial period, birthplace of the Champaran Satyagraha. Today, one of India's fastest-growing cities and the capital of a state of 130 million people.


The festival venue will be announced in September 2026. Options under consideration include: the historic Gandhi Maidan amphitheatre, a purpose-built riverside pavilion on the Ganga ghats, and Patna's premier convention facilities. All evening SPIC MACAY concerts will take place in the open air, beside the river.


The Ganga Amphitheatre — our evening concert venue — will be set up directly on the ghats, with the Ganga as backdrop and the Patna skyline as witness. Under the stars. Beside the river. There is no more powerful cultural stage in all of India.

Ancient Pataliputra

The City That Made India

To attend the Ganga Literature Festival is to walk in the footsteps of Chanakya, Chandragupta, Ashoka, Megasthenes, and Fa Hien. Patna offers delegates an experience no other literature festival city can match — a living, breathing connection to the foundations of Indian civilisation.

Pataliputra
Once described by Megasthenes as surpassing all cities of the world in magnificence — the capital of the Maurya Empire and seat of Chanakya's statecraft.
Nalanda Legacy
60km away — the world's first residential university, where scholars from China, Korea, Central Asia and Persia came to learn from India's masters. Now reborn as Nalanda University.
Ganga Aarti
The daily evening aarti at Patna's ghats — an ancient ritual of light, sound, and devotion that has continued for thousands of years, uninterrupted.
Guru Gobind Singh's Patna
Takht Sri Harmandir Ji (Patna Sahib) — the birthplace of the tenth Sikh Guru, one of India's most sacred sites and a living symbol of Bihar's plural heritage.

Our Guiding Purpose

Viksit Bharat @2047

"A developed India is not just an economic goal. It is a civilisational sankalp."

Mananiya Pradhan Mantri Narendra Modi Ji's vision of Viksit Bharat — a fully developed India by 2047, the centenary of independence — is the most ambitious national project India has undertaken since the freedom struggle itself. It envisions India as a $30 trillion economy, a global leader in technology and innovation, a nation where every citizen lives with dignity and opportunity, and a civilisation that has recovered its rightful place at the forefront of human progress.


The Ganga Literature Festival is dedicated to this sankalp. Every session, every conversation, every book, every debate at GLF is framed by a single question: how does this bring us closer to Viksit Bharat?

विकसित भारत — PM Modi's Vision in His Own Words

"By 2047, when India completes 100 years of independence, I want to see a developed India — not just in infrastructure and economy, but in the hearts and minds of its people. A nation confident of its past, clear-eyed about its present, and bold about its future."

GLF's Six Pillars of Viksit Bharat Discourse
Civilisational Confidence
A Viksit Bharat begins with a people who know who they are. GLF's civilisational sessions recover India's authentic intellectual, scientific, and artistic heritage — the foundation of national confidence.
Economic Transformation
From $3.5 trillion to $30 trillion requires enterprise, innovation, and the right policy environment. GLF brings together the authors and thinkers mapping this economic journey.
Strategic Security
A Viksit Bharat must be a secure Bharat. GLF's national security conversations examine the threats, the responses, and the resolve required to protect India's rise.
Social Justice & Constitutional Integrity
Viksit Bharat must be Nyayik Bharat — a just India where constitutional rights are guaranteed equally to all citizens, and where historical wrongs are honestly confronted.
Cultural Regeneration
India's classical arts, languages, and traditions are not relics. They are living resources for innovation, wellbeing, and national identity. SPIC MACAY's presence at GLF embodies this conviction.
Bihar & Eastern India's Rise
Viksit Bharat cannot be achieved without the development of Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Jharkhand, and Eastern India. GLF is born here, and committed to this conversation above all others.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is GLF a free festival?
GLF has a nominal registration fee designed to cover logistics and ensure committed attendance. Student rates (₹299) ensure accessibility for all.
Can I bring my school or college group?
Absolutely — and we actively encourage it. Contact institutional@gangalitfest.org for group rates and programmes designed for young audiences.
Will sessions be recorded and available online?
Select sessions will be live-streamed and made available on BIHAAN and BluOne Ink's YouTube channels. The SPIC MACAY concerts will not be broadcast — they must be experienced in person.
Is GLF affiliated with a political party?
GLF is an independent cultural and literary festival. It is guided by the values of civilisational pride, intellectual honesty, and national purpose — values that belong to all Indians, not any party.
How can I propose a session or suggest a speaker?
We welcome suggestions from the public. Write to programme@gangalitfest.org with your proposal. While we cannot accommodate every suggestion, every submission is read.
